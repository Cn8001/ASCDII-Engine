Animation

Any property you can edit in the Inspector can be animated. Clips are sampled from scene time, so scrubbing is exact and playback never drifts.

Concepts

  • A clip belongs to an object and has a play mode: once, loop or pingpong.
  • A track animates one property, such as pos.z, color or glow. Numbers, vectors and colors blend smoothly; switches change at the key.
  • A key is a value at a time, with an ease toward the next key: linear, in, out, inout, step, back or elastic.

Tutorial: a floating lantern

  1. Select a lantern and open the Animation tab (Ctrl+6).
  2. Press REC. While it is red, every change you make in the Inspector becomes a key at the playhead.
  3. Click the ruler at 0 to move the playhead there, then nudge pos z to 0. A clip appears with its first key.
  4. Click the ruler at 1.5 and set pos z to 2.
  5. Press REC again to stop recording, and press the play button in the Animation tab to preview. Switch the clip to pingpong so it bobs up and down.
  6. Click a key to select it and pick an ease, for example inout. Drag keys sideways to retime them; they snap to 0.05 seconds. The trash button deletes the selected key.
Without RECHover a property in the Inspector and click the diamond at the end of its row to key just that property. Double click a track lane to add a key at that time.

A window of its own

Press the float button at the right of the Animation header to turn it into a window you can move and resize over the view. The editor remembers where you left it. The arrow in its title bar docks it back.

Several clips per object

Press + Clip in the Animation tab to give the selection another clip, for example walk next to idle. Click a clip's header to make it the one new keys go to; it is highlighted. Double click the header to rename it.

Animator

An Animator component plays its object's clips as the states of a controller and switches between them when conditions hold.

  1. In the Project window choose Create, Animator Controller. The Animator window opens.
  2. Add parameters on the left: float, bool or trigger (true for one transition, then reset).
  3. Add states with + State; pick each state's clip on the right. The start state has an amber border; any state can be made the start.
  4. Shift drag from one state to another to add a transition, then give it conditions such as speed > 0.1, or tick After the clip ends. A transition from any state leaves every state, for hits and deaths.
  5. Add an Animator to the object and drag the controller onto its controller slot. The graph button on its card opens the controller.

While the game runs, the selected object's active state glows in the Animator window. With an animator running, the object's clips play only as its states; the timeline still previews them while you edit.

local anim = self:component("animator")
anim:set("speed", 3)
anim:trigger("jump")
log(anim.state)
anim:play("idle")   -- jump to a state

Animation from scripts

Scripts can start tweens at runtime, for doors, pickups and UI. They use the same easing and are never saved into the scene.

tween(self, "pos.z", 4, 0.6, "out")
