Models

Wavefront .obj and .fbx models from Blender or other 3D tools are drawn in Braille with lighting, shadows and fog, at any rotation.

Importing

  1. Export the model as .obj, with its materials as .mtl if it has any, or as .fbx (FBX 2011 or later, binary or ASCII) with its textures not embedded.
  2. Drag the .obj or .fbx into the Scene view. It is copied into the open folder together with its .mtl files and the textures they name, keeping their relative paths, and placed where it was dropped. An FBX brings each texture found at the path the file names, or next to the file under its own name.
  3. Further instances are created from GameObject, Model, by dragging the model tile from the Project window, or by a double click on it.

What is read

OBJ / MTLUsed for
v, fPositions and faces; faces with more than three corners are split into triangles.
vtTexture coordinates.
usemtl, mtllibWhich material each part uses.
KdThe part's color.
map_KdThe part's texture, imported as a texture asset.
FBXUsed for
MeshesPositions and polygons of every mesh in the file, each placed by its object's position, rotation and scale and those of its parents; polygons are split into triangles.
UV layerTexture coordinates, from the first UV set.
MaterialsWhich material each polygon uses; the material's diffuse color is the part's color.
Diffuse textureThe part's texture, imported as a texture asset.
Axes and unitsThe file's up and front axes and its unit; FBX units become meters, so one meter in the 3D tool is one world unit.

Animation, skinning, blend shapes, cameras and lights in an FBX are skipped; a rigged character appears in its bind pose.

Normals in the file are ignored; faces are shaded flat, which reads best at Braille resolution. A few hundred faces are sufficient for a building.

Import settings

Selecting a model tile shows its import settings in the Inspector:

SettingMeaning
upZOBJ only. Off for the common Y up export; on for files that are already Z up, as some CAD tools write them. An FBX names its own axes.
scaleWorld units per file unit. One world unit is one tile of ground; a door is about 2 units tall.

The model is turned so that its front, as seen in the 3D tool's front view, faces the default camera.

The Mesh component

PropertyMeaning
modelThe model asset.
materialOptional; replaces the model's own materials on every part that has no material of its own under Parts.
tint, emissiveColor multiplied over the model, and self lighting.
twoSidedDraws back faces as well, for open models or models with inconsistent face directions.
shadowA soft contact shadow on the ground.
PartsOne material slot per part of the model, named after its usemtl or FBX material, for example eyes. An empty slot keeps the part's own material.

The object's rotation, scale and flip apply to the model. A solid object blocks with its rotated bounds; a Mesh Collider blocks with the model's outline seen from above, see Physics.

Models with several materials

A character such as a figure with separate textures for its eyes, head and body is one model with several parts. In the 3D tool, each part is assigned its own material, and its faces are unwrapped onto that material's texture; the texture coordinates come with the file, so the engine does not unwrap models. The OBJ file then names each part's material with usemtl, and the MTL file gives that material its texture with map_Kd; an FBX file carries the same in its materials and their diffuse textures. Each part is drawn with its own texture and its own texture coordinates.

The Mesh card in the Inspector lists the parts under Parts, one material slot each. A part is drawn with the first of these that is set:

  1. its slot under Parts;
  2. the card's material, which applies to every part;
  3. its texture from the MTL or FBX file, tinted by its diffuse color;
  4. its diffuse color (Kd).

Dragging a material from the Project window onto a model in the Scene view fills the slot of the part under the pointer, and a message names the part; dragging it onto a slot in the Inspector works as for any material slot. tint and emissive apply on top of every part. Scripts set a slot by its full name:

local m = find("mario"):component("mesh")
m["parts/eyes"] = "materials/eyes_closed.material.json"
