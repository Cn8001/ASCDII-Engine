Textures and materials

Import a picture, put it in a material and drop the material on an object. The engine samples the texture for every Braille dot, then lights, shades and fogs it like everything else.

In three steps

  1. Drag a .png or .jpg into the Project window. It lands in textures/.
  2. Right click it and choose Create Material. The new material uses the texture and opens in the Inspector.
  3. Drag the material tile onto an object in the Scene view or in the Hierarchy, or onto any material slot in the Inspector.

Import settings

Click a texture to see its settings in the Inspector. Changes are saved in the texture's .meta and applied at once.

SettingDoes
maxSizeThe longest side kept, 256 by default. A Braille dot is coarse; small textures look the same and load faster.
filterlinear blends neighbouring texels, for photos and painted art. nearest keeps them crisp, for pixel art.
alphaCutoffTexels more transparent than this are holes, so a quad with a cut out picture shows only the picture.

Textures are mip mapped: a surface far away samples a smaller, averaged copy, so distant textures stay calm instead of flickering as the camera moves.

Materials

A material is a small .material.json asset. Create one with Create, Material and edit it in the Inspector:

PropertyDoes
albedoThe base color; it tints the texture, white leaves it as it is.
textureA texture slot; drag a texture tile onto it.
tilingX, tilingYHow many times the texture repeats across a face.
offsetX, offsetYSlides the texture.
emissiveLight the surface gives off itself, 0 to 1; it glows at night.
unlitIgnore lighting entirely.

The Cube, Plane, Quad and Mesh components have a material slot. A cube maps the texture once onto every face, a plane and a quad once onto their surface, and a model uses the texture coordinates of its file. Every object that uses a material changes when you edit it.

Convert to glyph art

Right click a texture and choose Convert to Art, or use the button under its preview. The picture becomes glyph art in art/, 48 cells wide: each cell shows two pixels stacked with a half block, which keeps the picture's proportions in a terminal cell. Use it as a sprite, for logos, portraits and signs that should look sharp rather than lit.
